# Paywalls, not PDF bugs, sink ratings

People give PDF apps one or two stars mostly because of how the apps charge, not because of how they handle PDFs. In about 1,780 substantive 1–2★ App Store and Google Play reviews of Acrobat, PDF Expert, Foxit, PDFelement and Xodo (2024–Nov 2026), **36–43% complain about paywalls, subscriptions or price**. In a smaller sample of about 98 Trustpilot 1–2★ reviews of web tools and e-signature services, **55–60% are about trials that turn into charges, auto-renewal, cancellation or refused refunds**. After money come updates that redesign the interface or remove features (16–19%), crashes (9–14%), lost work (8–12%) and forced sign-in (about 8%). AI buttons that can't be removed are a small but growing complaint (about 4%). Complaints about form filling and signing are only **about 2–3% of low reviews each**, but each one comes from someone who failed at the exact task they installed the app for. Those failures follow a few patterns: the app paywalls or watermarks the form halfway through, text can't be placed on flat forms, filled data disappears on save, and "sign" turns out to mean a picture rather than a certificate. National eID signing apps fail differently. Their worst ratings (AutoFirma iOS: **1.3★ from about 2,000 ratings**) come from plumbing failures: the browser can't reach the local helper, no certificate shows up, Java breaks, the card isn't read, and errors are cryptic. On Linux the evidence comes from bug trackers rather than store reviews, and it describes the same gap: forms that lose data, and smartcard signing that requires manual NSS setup. Most of these complaints are failures that pdfr's design already avoids. The main risks for pdfr are getting the details wrong in the free-key flow and promising signature validity it can't deliver for every recipient.

## About 40% of store complaints are about money, mostly money already paid

Users rarely object to paying as such. They object to losing what they already paid for, to learning the price only after doing the work, and to recurring billing for something they use now and then. The bitterest form is **"I paid once and now it's a subscription"**. A strict regex found 101 such reviews in the 2,145 low reviews, 42 of them for PDF Expert iOS alone. One reviewer wrote, "I paid $40 for this app. Somehow my app got updated, and now basic functionality like filling out forms is stuck behind a subscription paywall, so that they can sell you a bunch of AI garbage" ([Mac App Store, PDF Expert](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1055273043?see-all=reviews)). Another: "I made a one time purchase 10 years ago… the update revoked all editing capabilities without warning" ([App Store, PDF Expert](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id743974925?see-all=reviews)). A Foxit customer who paid "close to $250.00 per computer" for a perpetual licence reports features removed later ([App Store, Foxit](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id507040546?see-all=reviews)). After Xodo was sold, a reviewer said the new owners made the free tier "useless, with ubiquitous watermarks, ONE action per day" ([Google Play, Xodo](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.xodo.pdf.reader)). The same thing happens on Linux. Master PDF Editor's free v4 became a demo that watermarks edited output, and Mint users went looking for the old 4.3.89 builds whose download links no longer work ([Linux Mint forum](https://forums.linuxmint.com/viewtopic.php?t=394059)).

The second pattern is **do the work for free, then pay to save it**: 85 low reviews mention watermarks and 25 describe editing first and hitting the paywall at save. "BECAUSE I DID NOT PURCHASE THE APP AFTER 15 MINUTES, IT PUT WATERMARKS IN BLUE ACROSS EVERY PAGE OF THE DOCUMENT (100 PAGES)" ([Mac App Store, PDFelement](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1470732135?see-all=reviews)). "Wanna go through all the trouble of using the app and signing a PDF only to find out that you can't save the PDF unless you PAY?????" ([App Store, Acrobat](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id469337564?see-all=reviews)). The web form fillers depend on this model. **All four sampled DocFly 1★ reviews** describe it: "Advertises itself as free and then asks you to pay in the end after you spend the time to fill out your PDF" ([Trustpilot, DocFly](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/docfly.com)). A pdfFiller reviewer says, "its a free pdf editor, which is not free" ([Trustpilot, pdfFiller](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/pdffiller.com?stars=1)).

On web and e-signature services, the problems are mostly about how billing works. **About 22 of the 98 sampled reviews** describe a free trial that turned into a yearly charge. Smallpdf accounts for 10 of its 15, for example "they charge you £81 without any clear warning" ([Trustpilot, Smallpdf](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/smallpdf.com?stars=1)). Adobe charges early-cancellation fees ("they charged me AUD$196!!!") ([Trustpilot, Adobe](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/adobe.com?stars=1)). The FTC sued Adobe in June 2024 over a fee of 50% of the remaining contract that was "buried … in small print"; the notes did not verify how the case ended ([FTC](https://ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/06/ftc-takes-action-against-adobe-executives-hiding-fees-preventing-consumers-easily-cancelling)). Ratings show the damage: Adobe's company-wide Trustpilot score is **1.1/5 with 91% one-star** and DocuSign's is **2.8 with 69% one-star**, while store averages stay at 4.4–4.7 because satisfied users rarely write reviews ([Trustpilot, Adobe](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/adobe.com?stars=1); [Trustpilot, DocuSign](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/docusign.com?stars=1)). Many of these complainants were one-off users ("used it once", "for a medical payment"). They had a single task, and a subscription was the wrong product for them.

| Theme | Share of low reviews | Where it hits hardest |
|---|---|---|
| Paywall / subscription / price | 43% Apple, 36% Play | PDF Expert iOS 52%, PDFelement iOS 50% |
| Trial-to-charge, renewal, cancel, refund (web/e-sign) | ~55–60% of Trustpilot sample | Smallpdf, Adobe, DocuSign, pdfFiller |

| Crash / bug / freeze | 14% Apple, 9% Play | Foxit iOS 38%, Xodo iOS 31% |

The Trustpilot figures are hand tallies of the first page of recent low reviews per service, not a representative sample ([App Store listings](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id469337564?see-all=reviews); [Google Play listings](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.adobe.reader)).

## Forced accounts, bloat and lost edits make up the second tier

Forced sign-in gets in the way of the task itself, more than it raises privacy worries. "Tried to open the app on a plane without internet and it doesn't even open" ([App Store, Acrobat](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id469337564?see-all=reviews)). "The process of signing-in is so important to the company that they are ready to close the application without letting a guy do his job on a PDF file before a deadline" ([Google Play, Acrobat](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.adobe.reader)). A PDF Expert Mac user found the app "can't close it until you sign into/up for an account" and could only quit it through Activity Monitor ([Mac App Store, PDF Expert](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1055273043?see-all=reviews)). Explicit privacy complaints are **under 1%** of store reviews and essentially absent from the Trustpilot sample. When privacy does come up, it comes through cloud defaults, as in "if you sign medical documents on here, your social security number is now publicly visible" ([Google Play, Acrobat](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.adobe.reader)). Angry reviewers write about money and access. Privacy probably sways purchases more than it sways ratings. Reddit, where privacy talk would most likely show up, could not be reached in this research.

Redesigns and clutter account for the "used to love it" reviews: "Interface is a junkyard, I don't want an account, AI, or have to dig through menus to find a clean read mode" ([App Store, PDF Expert](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id743974925?see-all=reviews)). One reviewer summed up the AI complaint in five words: "I don't need AI I need rotate" ([App Store, Foxit](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id507040546?see-all=reviews)). Losing work is the most serious complaint in this tier: "literally made me fail my midterm … constantly deletes the work and edits i make" ([App Store, Xodo](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id805075929?see-all=reviews)). Two reviewers describe a fillable PDF swelling from about 700 KB to 21–29 MB, with the size-reduction tool then behind the paywall ([App Store, PDF Expert](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id743974925?see-all=reviews); [Google Play, Foxit](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.foxit.mobile.pdf.lite)). Several reviewers say outright that the core product was good and the rating fell over trust: "Would been 5 but, I Hate who you've become" ([App Store, Acrobat](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id469337564?see-all=reviews)).

## Form filling breaks at three points: the paywall, flat pages, and the save

Among form complaints, almost nobody writes about field detection. They complain that the task got paywalled or watermarked halfway through, that typed text can't be placed or re-edited on non-fillable forms, or that data vanished on save. The first is the most frequent: "Why on gods green earth is putting BLANK TEXT BOXES onto my PDF locked behind a PAYWALL" ([App Store, PDF Expert](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id743974925?see-all=reviews)). On forms specifically, a watermark is worse than ugly, because it "can make docs like filling in a form look suspect to those receiving it" ([Google Play, Xodo](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.xodo.pdf.reader)).

Flat and scanned forms are where pdfr's auto-detection fits. "Turns out if it wasn't set up as a form to begin with, this app is useless" ([Google Play, PDFelement](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.wondershare.pdfelement)). "I just wanted to fill out ONE simple non-fillable PDF file which seems too much to ask in this day and age" ([Google Play, Foxit](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.foxit.mobile.pdf.lite)). The specific complaints are about placement and editing: "once you have typed something, you cannot move the text box nor you can edit it again" (Foxit); "You drag to a location and it will stick to an entirely different column" (PDFelement) ([App Store, PDFelement](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1516765045?see-all=reviews)); "you need to hope it will detect the correct size of the text box, it never does" (PDF Expert) ([App Store, PDF Expert](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id743974925?see-all=reviews)). Font size gets the next most complaints: "Almost impossible to change FONT SIZE … This took all morning to do a form I should have been able to complete in 15 minutes" ([Mac App Store, PDF Expert](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1055273043?see-all=reviews)). The sample contained **no complaints about comb fields or baseline misalignment**. Users may describe these problems vaguely, or they may simply be rare.

Data loss on save and flatten is the most damaging form failure: "export it with flatten ... Aaand all the info I painstakingly put in is gone … this has like 10 AI buttons but no save button" ([Google Play, PDFelement](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.wondershare.pdfelement)); "save a 'flattened' copy that is completely blank" ([App Store, Xodo](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id805075929?see-all=reviews)). One PDFelement user found the app had overwritten the original unfilled form, which was now "fully filled out WITH the watermark" ([Google Play, PDFelement](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.wondershare.pdfelement)). Linux has the same cluster in its bug trackers. Okular has at least eight form-saving bugs, among them form data that Acrobat can't read and fields that don't print. A confirmed 2025 bug shows that saving a form "leaks history information", because the file "contains all intermediate text versions" ([KDE bug 499471](https://bugs.kde.org/show_bug.cgi?id=499471); [KDE bug 393206](https://mail.kde.org/pipermail/okular-devel/2018-April/028133.html)). Linux workarounds for flat forms make things worse. Adding a signature image with Xournal++, LibreOffice Draw or GIMP "seems to disable or remove the form fields" ([Arch forum](https://bbs.archlinux.org/viewtopic.php?id=254007)).

XFA forms are the one gap that no app is going to close. Okular's "XFA Forms are not supported" ticket has **12 duplicates and 27 CC'd users**. Government and immigration forms account for much of the demand, and the maintainer says XFA is "99.98% never going to be supported unless you come by a few hundred thousands euros" ([KDE bug 263042](https://bugs.kde.org/show_bug.cgi?id=263042)). Store reviewers report the same with JavaScript forms: "All but the most basic forms use JavaScript" ([App Store, Xodo](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id805075929?see-all=reviews)). Evince shows US I-9 and N-400 forms as a "Please wait..." page ([GNOME bug 687951](https://bugzilla.gnome.org/show_bug.cgi?id=687951)). XFA keeps some users dual-booting. Adobe is "about the only reason I still dual boot Windows alongside Linux" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=13379493)).

## Signing has two audiences, and certificate users are the angrier one

Consumers want a free drawn or typed signature that survives export. Their complaints are paywalls ("$40 to sign a pdf"), signatures that disappear ("When I export to iCloud or email, the signature gets removed"), and crashes mid-signature ([App Store, PDF Expert](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id743974925?see-all=reviews); [Google Play, Acrobat](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.adobe.reader)). Professionals want real certificate signatures and are furious when "sign" means a picture: "what they mean is you can put a picture that acts as a proxy for your signature. It does not mean that you can use a certificate to provide a digital signature." A US CAC user wrote "DO NOT BUY IF YOU NEED CAC SIGNATURE ABILITY", was refused a refund about 45 days after buying, and was out about $140 ([Mac App Store, PDF Expert](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1055273043?see-all=reviews)). Indian users with Aadhaar or government certificates complain that a valid signature shows "verified but … green tick missing" ([Google Play, Foxit](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.foxit.mobile.pdf.lite)). Complaints about e-signature services are mostly about the person receiving the request: expired links, OTP codes rejected as invalid, signatures not showing in preview ([Trustpilot, DocuSign](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/docusign.com?stars=1)). Courts and agencies sometimes reject plain e-signatures and require wet ink or a certificate-based digital signature ([Cryptomathic](https://www.cryptomathic.com/blog/us-court-rejects-docusign-e-signatures-as-method-to-provide-digital-authorization); [Maine.gov](https://www.maine.gov/future/sites/maine.gov.dafs.bbm.procurementservices/files/inline-files/Digital%20Signature%20Tools_Quick%20Reference%20Guide.pdf)).

National eID signing apps fail before the signature is even made. The AutoFirma iOS app is rated **1.3/5 from about 2,000 ratings**: "Half the time it doesn't work and you can't tell why" ([App Store ES](https://apps.apple.com/es/app/cliente-firma-movil/id627410001?see-all=reviews)). On desktop, the defining error is "No se pudo contactar con AutoFirma" ("could not contact AutoFirma"): the browser launches the helper, but the helper never starts its local WebSocket server. Issue #416 has been open since October 2024 and covers six macOS browsers. Users write "Imposible hacerlo desde Mac" ("impossible from a Mac") and "Los logs no muestran nada" ("the logs show nothing") ([GitHub #416](https://github.com/ctt-gob-es/clienteafirma/issues/416)). In 2026, AutoFirma showed no certificates from the macOS Keychain because the user had marked the personal certificate "Always Trust" ([GitHub #520](https://github.com/ctt-gob-es/clienteafirma/issues/520)). On Linux, AutoFirma's own manual warns that the GUI may not open on distros that ship a headless JRE ([AutoFirma manual](https://firmaelectronica.gob.es/content/dam/firmaelectronica/ciudadanos/descargas/pdf/AF-manual-instalacion-usuarios-ES%20v1-9.pdf)). In Germany, AusweisApp users report that when the phone fails to read the card over NFC, the app shows a false wrong-PIN lockout ([Google Play, AusweisApp](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.governikus.ausweisapp2&hl=en&gl=de)). With the Belgian eID, Acrobat reports "Unsupported algorithm". The fix is to unlink Acrobat's PKCS#11 module, and on macOS 15.6 that leaves "no certificate shown to use to sign" ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/acrobat-reader-discussions/niet-ondersteund-algoritme-unsupported-algoritme/m-p/15500537/highlight/true)). Portugal's Autenticação.gov has PIN dialogs that crash or never appear on macOS 27, no native Apple Silicon build, and a Flatpak that segfaults in fontconfig ([GitHub amagovpt issues](https://github.com/amagovpt/autenticacao.gov/issues)). The eID numbers are qualitative: no store ratings were found for the Belgian, Estonian, Portuguese or Italian apps. Still, AusweisApp's **4.5★ across 76K reviews** shows that card-reading apps can be rated well when the basic job works.

Linux PDF readers add NSS on top. Okular reads certificates only from an NSS database, so users register tokens with `modutil` and point Okular at a Firefox profile. Some repeated "PIN" prompts turn out to be asking for the Firefox master password ([gregbur.me](https://gregbur.me/2022/04/28/deep-dive-digitally-signing-pdfs-with-okular/)). KDE bug 459173, open since 2022, describes Okular asking for the smartcard PIN "in an infinite loop, without actually applying a signature" with Belgian, Luxembourgish and Spanish eIDs. These cards carry two certificates, and one reporter fixed the loop only by deleting the non-repudiation certificate from the card ([KDE bug 459173](https://bugs.kde.org/show_bug.cgi?id=459173)). The deciding test is whether Acrobat accepts the output. "I would not be able to share signed documents with my colleagues who use non-open source tools," wrote a user whose Okular-signed PDFs locked in Acrobat ([KDE bug 461371](https://bugs.kde.org/show_bug.cgi?id=461371)). Another bug report has correct QES signatures showing in Okular as "changed after signature" while Acrobat called them valid ([KDE bug 496490](https://bugs.kde.org/show_bug.cgi?id=496490)). As recently as 2026, GNOME Papers' signing depended on the Firefox certificate database, and visual and digital signatures could not yet be combined ([Papers visual signatures](https://lbaudin.pages.gitlab.gnome.org/blog/posts/signatures-in-papers/)). The fallbacks Linux users name are JSignPdf on Java 8, Foxit or Adobe in Wine, dual-booting, and print-sign-scan ([Manjaro forum](https://forum.manjaro.org/t/linux-pdf-signing-software/28519); [Lemmy thread](https://scribe.disroot.org/post/6747621/8973941)).

## What pdfr should learn

Nearly every major complaint maps onto a design choice pdfr has already made, so most of the work is to keep those choices intact and say them out loud. The table splits the findings into three groups: what to avoid, what to put in marketing, and complaints pdfr can win on.

| | Finding from the reviews | What it means for pdfr |
|---|---|---|
| **Avoid** | Paywall or watermark revealed after the work is done (85 watermark and 25 pay-to-save complaints; all DocFly 1★s) | Never watermark, and never block saving in the personal tier. If the free key is required for anything, ask for it before the user starts, not at save. If getting the key needs an email address, make that obvious up front, so the email request isn't read as the "trap to get your email" seen in pdfFiller reviews |
| **Avoid** | Taking back features people paid for (101 "used to / already paid" reviews; Master PDF Editor v4) | Treat "lifetime" and "one-time" as binding promises. Never move a feature from the free key or the $39 licence into a higher tier. Publish what each tier covers and keep older builds downloadable |
| **Avoid** | Forced sign-in and online checks (~8%; "on a plane", "before a deadline") | Keep licence checks fully offline (Ed25519 verification does this). Never put a login or a network check in the way of opening or saving |
| **Avoid** | Update churn, clutter and AI buttons that won't go away (16–19%, ~4%) | Keep the interface focused and stable between releases. Anything new must be easy to hide, and update notes should say what moved |
| **Avoid** | Lost edits, overwritten originals, blank flattened copies, file bloat, leaked edit history | Make saving the most-tested path. Save a new file by default or ask before overwriting, check the output after flattening, keep file size close to the original, and don't leave earlier text revisions in incremental saves |
| **Avoid** | "Sign" that turns out to be a picture (the PDF Expert CAC refund case) | In marketing and in the UI, separate a visible signature from a PAdES certificate signature. Never call an image a digital signature |
| **Emphasise** | Money themes make up ~40% of store and ~55–60% of Trustpilot low reviews | Lead with: no subscription, no trial that charges your card, nothing to cancel, $39 once for business, free for personal use, with no watermark and nothing locked at save |
| **Emphasise** | Account and offline complaints; latent privacy worry | "No account. Works offline. Your documents never leave your computer." Present it mainly as being able to get the job done anywhere, with privacy second |
| **Emphasise** | Linux users stuck with Wine, dual-boot, JSignPdf on Java, NSS wiring | "Sign with your ID card on Linux without Java, Wine, browser plug-ins or NSS setup." Name the supported cards and middleware (OpenSC, beid, pteid via p11-kit), the browser stores and .p12 files |
| **Opportunity** | Flat or scanned forms the incumbents can't fill; text boxes that drift, can't be moved or default to Courier 18pt | Auto-detection is a feature nobody else is advertising. Show a flat government form filled in seconds in demos. Make sure text can be moved, resized and re-edited after typing, and that font sizes fit the box |
| **Opportunity** | eID plumbing failures: no certificate visible, PIN loops on two-certificate cards, false lockouts, cryptic codes, empty logs | Show every certificate from p11-kit, NSS and PKCS#12 without filtering on trust settings, and handle two-certificate cards with one clear choice. Show retries left before PIN entry. Replace error codes with plain diagnoses such as "pcscd isn't running" or "no PKCS#11 module for this card; install opensc/beid" |
| **Opportunity** | Acrobat acceptance is the real test (Okular lock and "changed after signing" bugs; Foxit missing green tick) | Test pdfr's PAdES output in Acrobat for each supported card and publish the results. Embedding revocation data and a timestamp is what earns the green tick. Visible and digital signatures in one step is something GNOME Papers can't yet do |
| **Opportunity** | XFA government forms push users back to Windows | Don't promise XFA support. Detect XFA or dynamic forms and say plainly what pdfr can and can't do, for example by offering to fill the flat rendering, rather than showing a "Please wait..." page |

## Conclusion

The research changes how pdfr should think about competing. Its rivals' weakness is less their PDF engines than their business models and the plumbing around signing, and that weakness has grown since 2024, as one-time products became subscriptions and Linux eID users were left with Java helpers and NSS wiring. pdfr's pricing, offline licence and native PKCS#11 stack answer the largest complaint categories before any feature comparison starts. That cuts both ways: the "used to love it" reviews show that trust built on pricing can be lost in a single update. The free lifetime key and the $39 licence are pdfr's strongest marketing claims only for as long as no later release narrows them.

The most valuable niche is users whose problems the big apps don't solve at all: people with flat or scanned government forms, and people who must produce a certificate signature from a national ID card on Linux or macOS. They write the angriest reviews because nothing else works for them, and they pay for anything that does: Master PDF Editor at about $70–85, PDF Studio, paid "DNIe para Mac" apps ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/master-pdf-editor/about); [GitHub #520](https://github.com/ctt-gob-es/clienteafirma/issues/520)). Some questions remain open. No Flathub, Snap or Reddit review text could be collected, there are no direct willingness-to-pay figures, and the share of these users who need XFA is unknown. Those gaps call for a few interviews with eID-on-Linux users rather than more scraping of reviews.
