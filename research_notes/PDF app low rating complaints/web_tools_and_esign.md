# Low-star review complaints: web/subscription PDF tools and e-signature services

**Method and sample (read this first).** Trustpilot 1-star (and some 2-star) review pages were fetched for Smallpdf, iLovePDF, pdfFiller, Adobe, DocuSign, SignNow, Dropbox Sign/HelloSign, Sejda and DocFly in November 2026. The sample is the most recent first page of low-star reviews on each profile: about 98 low-star excerpts in total (Smallpdf 15, iLovePDF 15, pdfFiller 13, Adobe 13, DocuSign 13+3 two-star, SignNow 11, HelloSign about 10, Sejda 4, DocFly 4). Almost all are from 2025–2026; the HelloSign and DocFly items are older and labelled. The quotes were extracted by a fetch tool that summarises pages. They are short and should be verbatim, but spot-check them before publishing. Theme counts are my own tallies of that sample: rough, and not statistically representative. **Not obtained:** G2 and Capterra (Capterra returned HTTP 403; G2 was not attempted, since it usually blocks automated access), Reddit (the search tool refused the domain), BBB. So the Reddit and "privacy" voice is under-sampled (see Gaps).

## Q1. Top recurring reasons for 1–2 star reviews

### Takeaway
Billing mechanics dominate overwhelmingly: a free trial that becomes an annual charge, auto-renewal without warning, cancellation that fails or costs a fee, and refunds refused for unused service. Together these make up roughly 55–60% of the sampled low-star reviews. Next come unreachable support (about 15%), a "free" tool that paywalls the download after the work is done (about 10%, and dominant for form fillers such as pdfFiller and DocFly), and output quality or layout problems (about 8–10%). The services' high overall scores (Smallpdf 4.8, iLovePDF 4.9) hide a small but very angry billing-driven 1-star tail.

### Inferences
- The 1-star tail is mainly about the business model rather than the PDF features: about 55–60% of the sampled reviews concern trial, renewal, cancellation or refund issues. A one-time purchase with no auto-renewing card-on-file trial would remove the single largest complaint category outright.
- "Annual charged up front at trial end" is the specific Smallpdf and iLovePDF pattern. "Annual plan billed monthly plus an early termination fee" is the Adobe and DocuSign pattern. "Free until you try to download" is the form-filler pattern (DocFly, pdfFiller).
- Many complainants are one-off users ("used it once", "for a medical payment"), so the job was a single task, not an ongoing need.

### Gaps
- No G2 or Capterra rating numbers or quotes were obtained (403 blocks). G2 and Capterra mostly hold business reviews and tend to skew higher than Trustpilot. This is unverified here.
- No BBB complaint data.
- Watermark complaints did not appear in the sample, perhaps because free tiers of these services mostly use task limits rather than watermarks. Not confirmed.

## Q2. How prominent are privacy and upload concerns?

### Takeaway
Explicit privacy complaints ("I don't want to upload my tax return") were essentially absent from the approximately 98 sampled Trustpilot 1–2 star reviews. Angry reviewers write about money, not data. Privacy shows up instead as account and email harvesting complaints, phishing worries around e-sign emails, and in vendor and security-blog guidance. Privacy is probably a latent purchase driver more than a review-rating driver, but Reddit, where that sentiment likely lives, could not be accessed.

### Cited Findings
- No visible 1–2 star Sejda review mentions privacy — [Trustpilot Sejda](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/sejda.com)
- The closest data-capture complaint: "This website is trap to get your email!" (pdfFiller, Nov 9, 2026) — [Trustpilot pdfFiller](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/pdffiller.com?stars=1)
- Accidental account creation through e-sign: "I was making a medical payment and … I inadvertently signed up an account with docusign." (Oct 22, 2026) — [Trustpilot DocuSign](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/docusign.com?stars=1)
- Trust and phishing in e-sign: "they don't tell you who it is from." (DocuSign 2-star, Jan 23, 2025); "I have no access to verify if the email is genuine." (Sep 16, 2026) — [Trustpilot DocuSign 2-star](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.docusign.com?stars=2). HelloSign reviewers report unsolicited invoices or signing requests they did not start (May 2, 2025) — [Trustpilot HelloSign](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/hellosign.com)
- Security and vendor guidance lists tax returns among files not to upload to online tools whose processing and retention can't be verified, and points to breach exposure (SSN, income, employer). Note that these are mostly vendor and SEO pages with a commercial interest — [PlatoForms](https://www.platoforms.com/blog/files-never-upload-online/); [Norton](https://us.norton.com/blog/security-tips/send-tax-documents-securely); [TurboTax FAQ](https://ttlc.intuit.com/questions/2565836-how-can-i-protect-the-tax-files-on-my-computer)

### Inferences
- For an offline desktop app, "your files never leave your computer" is a positioning message aimed at the privacy-conscious buyer. It is not a fix for what makes 1-star reviewers angry. The anti-subscription and no-paywall message addresses the review-site anger directly.

### Gaps
- Reddit (r/privacy, r/sysadmin, r/personalfinance) was unreachable because the search tool blocks reddit.com, so I have no verbatim Reddit privacy quotes. A follow-up with a browser or other access is needed.

## Q3. Complaints specific to FORM FILLING and to SIGNING

### Takeaway
Form-filling complaints centre on doing the work and then being paywalled at download, lost filled data, and broken fillable-form creation (pdfFiller, DocFly). Signing complaints in reviews centre on recipient-side friction (expired links, documents not arriving, patients struggling to fill forms, signatures not showing) and on lock-in. Legal-validity and certificate complaints did not appear in the sampled reviews. They show up instead in legal and court sources: wet-signature requirements, notarisation, and agency discretion.

### Form filling — Cited Findings
- Paywall after filling: "asks you to pay in the end after you spend the time to fill out your PDF" (DocFly, Jun 2025) — [Trustpilot DocFly](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/docfly.com)
- Lost work: "lost my pdf i had filled! waste of time" (pdfFiller, Oct 27, 2026) — [Trustpilot pdfFiller](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/pdffiller.com?stars=1)
- Fillable-form creation errors: "Tried to create a fillable form … The app made loads of errors, that I spent ages manually correcting." (pdfFiller, Oct 20, 2026) — same
- Field and text placement: iLovePDF "THE TEXTS ARE MISPLACED"; Sejda text boxes invisible in other readers ("PDF reader didn't show to text boxes") — [iLovePDF](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/ilovepdf.com?stars=1); [Sejda](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/sejda.com)
- Recipient form filling in e-sign: "Our patients struggled to fill out their forms" (SignNow, Aug 19, 2026) — [Trustpilot SignNow](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/signnow.com?stars=1)

### Signing — Cited Findings
- Expired links: "THE 'LINK' HAD EXPIRED so I was unable to do so!!" (DocuSign, Oct 20, 2026) — [Trustpilot DocuSign](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/docusign.com?stars=1)
- Delivery: "Clients don't receive the document." (DocuSign 2-star, Nov 3, 2026) — [Trustpilot DocuSign 2-star](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.docusign.com?stars=2)
- Signatures not rendering: "Often times the sigs would not show in Preview Mode, even though you know you placed them on the form"; and the upload rejection "The pdf contains content that is not allowed" (DocuSign 2-star, Aug 21, 2026) — same
- Sender unable to send: "I was unable to send out time-sensitive documents for signature" (HelloSign, Jul 6, 2024) — [Trustpilot HelloSign](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/hellosign.com)
- Account and OTP failure: "i receive the code and it said invalid" (DocuSign, Nov 3, 2026) — [Trustpilot DocuSign](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/docusign.com?stars=1)
- Scam abuse of the platform: "Knowingly aiding scammers. Refused to do anything about it." (SignNow, Mar 2, 2026) — [Trustpilot SignNow](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/signnow.com?stars=1)
- Legal validity, from non-review sources:
  - A US bankruptcy trustee argued that DocuSign is not an original "wet" signature, and the court sanctioned the attorney — [Cryptomathic](https://www.cryptomathic.com/blog/us-court-rejects-docusign-e-signatures-as-method-to-provide-digital-authorization); [BiggerPockets thread](https://biggerpockets.com/forums/51/topics/765675-us-court-rejects-docusign-e-signatures-as-method-to-provide-digit)
  - A 2016 Sacramento case invalidated DocuSign-only signatures where wet ink was required. This is a secondary source (Wikipedia) and the underlying document was not found — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/DocuSign)
  - A state document says agency heads decide when an e-signature suffices, and require a certificate-based digital signature otherwise — [Maine.gov](https://www.maine.gov/future/sites/maine.gov.dafs.bbm.procurementservices/files/inline-files/Digital%20Signature%20Tools_Quick%20Reference%20Guide.pdf)
  - A bank refused a DocuSign beneficiary form over receipt timing — [DocuSign Community](https://community.docusign.com/esignature-171/disputed-signature-on-a-beneficiary-form-24711)
  - Powers of attorney commonly need notarisation, which a plain e-signature does not satisfy — [OfficeConsumer](https://officeconsumer.com/can-a-poa-be-signed-via-docusign-w-examples-faqs/)
  - A California government user asks whether Adobe Sign meets Gov. Code 17201; the thread is unresolved — [Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/questions-35/is-adobe-sign-compliant-with-california-g-c-17201)

### Inferences
- For a desktop app, the signing pain points that matter are: (1) no recipient account, link expiry or OTP friction when the user signs their own document locally; (2) certificate-based (PAdES/PKCS#11) digital signatures that agencies demanding "digital, not electronic" signatures can accept; (3) signatures that render in every viewer, avoiding the "doesn't show in other readers" failure.

### Gaps
- No review-site quotes were found on certificate errors, such as Acrobat's "validity unknown" banner, or on EU qualified-signature rejection. These likely live on Reddit and Adobe forums, which I did not reach. No Acrobat Sign-specific Trustpilot page was sampled; Adobe's page mixes all products.

## Q4. What reviewers wanted instead

### Takeaway
The stated wishes are implicit rather than explicit: pay only for what I need right now, a clear warning before charging, a cooling-off period or easy refund, a free tool that is actually free to download, and a human to contact. Explicit "I want offline" or "one-time purchase" wording was rare in the sample.

### Cited Findings
- Pay per task, not a subscription: "I thought I was just paying for what I needed right then, not signing up for some yearly subscription." — [Trustpilot iLovePDF](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/ilovepdf.com?stars=1)
- A cooling-off period: "without allowing a reasonable cooling-off period" — [Trustpilot Smallpdf](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/smallpdf.com?stars=1)
- Notice before charging: "I received no notification before the charge." (Oct 9, 2026) — same
- Honest "free": "Information on web states free but it…" (title) — same; and "its a free pdf editor, which is not free." — [pdfFiller](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/pdffiller.com?stars=1)
- A human contact: "There is no way to contact a real person" — [Adobe](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/adobe.com?stars=1)
- Control of their documents without lock-in: "afraid to lose our documents" — [HelloSign](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/hellosign.com)

### Inferences
- A one-time licence for an offline app with no trial-to-subscription conversion directly answers Themes A–D and F, which make up about 70% of the sample. Messaging such as "no subscription, no card to start, nothing to cancel, your documents stay on your disk" maps one-to-one onto the top complaints.

### Gaps
- No direct quotes asking for "offline" or "desktop" were found. Reddit and G2 "alternatives considered" data would be needed to confirm demand.
